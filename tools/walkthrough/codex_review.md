**4. Convergence and changes to the method**

**Early improvement, followed by a plateau with oscillating trade-offs.** Across the nine aspects, rounds 3–5 average approximately **7.85 → 7.89 → 7.85**; meanwhile concision falls **7 → 7 → 6.33**, while facts improve **8 → 8 → 8.67**. That pattern is consistent with adding qualifications and evidence to satisfy factual judges while making the presentation harder to consume—not convergence toward every aspect exceeding eight.

I would change the loop as follows:

1. **Freeze the candidate.** Give each review a file hash, screenshots, and check results from that exact version. The mid-review change I observed demonstrates why this matters.
2. **Anchor the rubric in audience tasks.** Can a CMO explain the benefit, a CFO reproduce the economics, a creative director distinguish evidence from hypotheses, and an engineer explain the operating boundary?
3. **Separate hard failures from scored preferences.** Incorrect arithmetic, unsupported guarantees, missing output, and broken interactions should block acceptance; typography preferences should not carry equivalent weight.
4. **Assign distinct judge responsibilities.** Use stakeholder comprehension, repository fidelity, and rendered UX/accessibility reviews, with evidence required for deductions. Three general-purpose scores are not three independent forms of validation.
5. **Give the editor a bounded change hypothesis.** For example: “Reduce sheets 02 and 10 by 35%, preserving these five essential facts.” Re-score against the previous version without exposing its scores, and reject regressions in required content.
6. **Strengthen checks around meaning and rendering.** Validate figures from structured data, required exhibit content, per-source quotations, link targets, visible-word budgets, mobile labels, SVG opacity, and crop bounds; retain human review for semantic support and creative judgment.
7. **Stop on demonstrated usability and stability.** Require no hard failures, successful audience tasks, and no material regressions across two reviews—not repeated score-seeking until every average happens to clear eight.
